WorkRadar

01Problem statement

An engineering manager on Airtribe’s Learning Platform team starts every day by rebuilding the state of the team from four tools. The signals that matter are a P0 bug stopping learners from joining tonight’s live class, a designer blocked for two days, and a launch go/no-go waiting on the manager’s reply. They sit beside hundreds of FYIs and bot updates, and each tool marks priority differently, or not at all. Triage eats the first hour, blockers surface a day late, and escalations get missed.

How might we give a manager one trustworthy, prioritised answer to “what needs me today?”, so they can act in five minutes instead of searching for an hour?

WorkRadar ranks every item with a transparent score, uses AI to write the brief and triage unstructured threads, and lets the manager assign, finish or snooze work without switching tools.

02Target users

Primary: engineering managers and tech leads at Airtribe with 5–12 reports, such as Learning Platform, Growth and Data. Their work runs to fixed external deadlines: live classes, submission deadlines and launches.

Secondary: program and product leaders who coordinate cross-team launches like the GenAI Launchpad, and who need status and decisions without pinging the manager.

Not for v1: individual contributors, and directors who need cross-team roll-ups.

05Key pain points

Work is fragmented

Four tools, four priority systems. Jira has P-levels. Slack and email have none, yet hold the most urgent asks.

“Waiting on me” is invisible

Decisions, reviews and replies addressed to the manager get buried in threads and inboxes.

Blockers hide across tools

A PR waits on a ticket, which waits on another team. Nobody sees the chain until someone complains.

Urgency depends on time

A 7 pm live class or a same-day go/no-go matters more than a “P1” due next week. Tools don’t rank by deadline.

Noise drowns signal

Bots, Dependabot, FYIs and CC chains make up most of the volume and train managers to skim.

Black-box AI isn’t trusted

A summary with no reasons can’t be acted on. Managers need to see why something is urgent.

07MVP scope

In scope (built and working in the demo)

  • Four sources: Slack, Jira, GitHub, Gmail (realistic mock data, n8n ingestion)
  • Today dashboard: radar, AI brief, needs attention, who’s blocked
  • Priority feed with an explainable 0–100 score and three rings
  • Item detail, plain-language search, ⌘K palette, Team view
  • Assign (AI-suggested owner), Mark done, Snooze, Undo
  • Gemini brief via n8n or a serverless route, with on-device fallback
  • Empty, error and outage states; dark mode; mobile layout
  • Settings: AI engine, n8n webhooks, live ranking weights

Out of scope (v1)

  • Live OAuth and write-back to source tools (actions logged to n8n)
  • Director roll-ups; weights learned from behaviour
  • Alerts beyond the morning Slack brief
  • Teams, Outlook, Linear, Notion; admin and SSO

08Assumptions

  1. Explainable beats clever. Managers will trust a deterministic score they can inspect and re-weight more than an opaque AI ranking. AI writes and triages; it doesn’t rank.
  2. Read-only access is acceptable. Airtribe security will approve least-privilege OAuth scopes if message bodies are kept no longer than 7 days and never used for training.
  3. The push builds the habit. A short Slack brief at 08:45 brings managers in daily better than a dashboard alone.
  4. Time is the strongest signal. Deadline proximity, who is waiting and what is blocked predict “needs me today” better than labels.
  5. The volume estimates hold. To validate with 5 manager interviews and a 2-week diary study before the pilot.
  6. Cost is negligible. Gemini Flash-class models and self-hosted n8n keep a brief under ₹1 and pilot running costs near zero.

09Features

Must-have MVP, shipped in the demo

  • Unified priority feed ranked by six weighted signals: urgency, impact, unblocks others, waiting on you, stuck, escalation.
  • AI daily brief: top 3 for today, what happened, needs attention, who’s blocked and how to unblock them.
  • “Why it’s on your radar”: score breakdown on every item, with adjustable weights.
  • Item detail: AI summary, next step, dependencies and source context.
  • Natural-language search (“blocked prs waiting on me”) and ⌘K.
  • One-click actions: Assign (AI-suggested owner), Done, Snooze, Undo.
  • Team blockers, sorted by who needs the manager first.
  • Graceful degradation: n8n → Gemini → on-device; outage banner.

Nice-to-have Next releases

  • Write-back: assign in Jira, request reviews in GitHub, reply in Slack.
  • AI reply drafts for asks waiting on the manager.
  • Learned weights from what gets opened, snoozed or reordered.
  • Calendar awareness: plan the top 3 around meetings and focus time.
  • 1:1 prep and weekly digest for each report: shipped, stuck, waiting.
  • Director roll-ups across teams, with launch risk heatmaps.
  • More sources: Linear, Notion, Teams, Outlook, PagerDuty.
  • Quiet hours and personal SLAs (“reply to leadership within 4h”).
